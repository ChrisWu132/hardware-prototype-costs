## Common questions this answers

### How much does it cost to make a prototype of an electronic product?
For these three: $1,100 for a 24-channel logic analyzer (15 units), $6,500 for a lidar line-following car (10 units), $30,000 for a swing-arm tracked robot (4 units). Most of the difference is the development fee, which follows the engineering work, not the unit count.

### Why is the price per unit so high in a small batch?
In batches of 5 to 20 units, Anvol's engineers see unit cost split roughly 40% parts, 40% circuit board and board assembly, 15% enclosure, 5% final assembly and testing. Board assembly is expensive because each run pays a fixed machine setup charge.

### What do these totals leave out?
Certification (none of the three went through FCC or CE; it is quoted separately when a product needs it), shipping and import duties (usually arranged by the customer once units leave the factory), and extras such as tutorials, training or a live demo.

### What does development cost for a simple device vs a robot?
As a rule of thumb from the same engineers: about $500 to $3,000 for a simple small device, about $10,000 to $20,000 for a larger robot. These are experience ranges, not quotes; compare against the three real projects above.

### What about 100 or 1,000 units?
Not published. None of these projects were made at that volume, and small-batch unit prices should not be stretched to it.
